Shane Bullis — My Testimony

Founder & Executive Director | Island Warriors Street Outreach Society

From the Streets to Serving the Streets — My Story of Addiction, Survival, Recovery, and Purpose

My name is Shane Bullis, and some people know me as Bullet.

Before I tell you what Island Warriors Street Outreach Society is all about, I want to tell you a little bit about the man behind it and why this work means so much to me.

I'm not somebody who learned about addiction, homelessness, trauma, or incarceration from reading a textbook. I lived it.

For nearly 10 years, I was lost in addiction, homelessness, violence, and the streets. I lived a life that nearly killed me more times than I can count.

I've been shot three times. I've jumped from a five-storey building. I've overdosed. I've sat behind jail walls. I've slept in places no human being should ever have to sleep. I've experienced the loneliness, shame, judgment, and hopelessness that so many of our unhoused relatives face every single day.

There was a time when I didn't believe I would ever make it out alive.

I was using fentanyl heavily, destroying myself, and hurting the people who loved me. I lost years of my life that I'll never get back.

But Creator had other plans for me.

Through recovery, ceremony, culture, prayer, and the love of people who refused to give up on me, I slowly began finding my way back.

My Indigenous culture helped save my life. The teachings of our ancestors, the Seven Grandfather Teachings, and walking the Red Road gave me something I had been missing for so many years.

Identity. Connection. Belonging. Purpose.

On July 22, 2024, I began a new chapter in my life. Today, I have over two years of sobriety, and I wake up every morning grateful for another opportunity to do something meaningful with the life I was given.

But getting sober wasn't the end of my journey.

It was the beginning of my responsibility.

Because I know what it's like to be that person sitting on the sidewalk while the world walks past you.

I know what it's like to be hungry, cold, addicted, and wondering whether anybody even cares if you live or die.

And I made myself a promise.

If I ever made it out of that darkness, I would go back and help others find their way out too.

That's why Island Warriors Street Outreach Society was born.

We are an Indigenous-led, peer-driven nonprofit organization built on lived experience, compassion, dignity, cultural connection, and community.

We're not here to judge people for where they're at in life.

We're here to meet them where they're at.

Whether somebody is struggling with addiction, living without a home, coming out of incarceration, dealing with trauma, or simply looking for somebody who understands, we want them to know they are not alone.

Through street outreach, food and clothing support, peer mentorship, recovery navigation, Indigenous cultural teachings, and community connections, we work to bridge the gaps between our most vulnerable relatives and the support they deserve.

We believe that healing begins with connection.

We believe that every human being deserves dignity.

And we believe that no person's past should determine their future.

I don't walk onto Pandora Street looking down on anybody.

I walk onto Pandora Street remembering that I was once the person sitting there.

The only difference between me and somebody still struggling is that I was given another chance, and somebody helped me believe I was worth saving.

Now it's my turn to give that same hope to somebody else.

Island Warriors isn't just an organization to me.

It's a promise.

A promise to the people we've lost.

A promise to the people still fighting.

A promise to our Elders, our youth, our families, and the generations coming behind us.

And a promise that as long as I have breath in my lungs, I will continue showing up for our relatives.

We don't do this work because we're better than anybody.

We do this work because we've been there.

We are living proof that people can change, that recovery is possible, and that even the darkest chapters of our lives can become the foundation for something beautiful.

My past almost destroyed me.

Today, that same past gives me the understanding, compassion, and purpose to help others.

I went from surviving on the streets to serving on the streets.

And if my story can give even one person the courage to hold on for another day, then everything I survived has a purpose.

My name is Shane Bullis.

I'm a father, a grandfather, a man in recovery, an Indigenous warrior, and the proud Founder and Executive Director of Island Warriors Street Outreach Society.

We are Island Warriors.

We stand for our people. We stand with our people. And we will never forget where we came from.
