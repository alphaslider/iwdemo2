Our Foundation & Purpose

Who We Are & Our Mission

Island Warriors Street Outreach Society (IWSO) is a peer-led, Indigenous-led grassroots nonprofit organization based in Victoria, British Columbia.

We are people with lived experience helping people. Our leadership and outreach are grounded in Indigenous values, culture, community, and the understanding that healing begins with connection. Many of our team members understand firsthand the realities of addiction, homelessness, incarceration, trauma, poverty, and recovery. We take those lived experiences and turn them into purpose—walking alongside people who may be facing the same struggles we once faced ourselves.

"We believe that lived experience is lived expertise."

Our mission is to provide peer-led, Indigenous-led outreach, support, advocacy, cultural connection, and recovery navigation to people impacted by homelessness, addiction, trauma, incarceration, poverty, and social isolation. We work to bridge gaps, restore hope and dignity, and meet people where they are to walk beside them toward where they want to be.

100% Peer & Indigenous Led

Ground-level boots on the street.

Our Vision

"A community where no one is forgotten and no one has to walk alone."

We envision Indigenous people and all community members having access to culturally safe, peer-led support that recognizes the whole person—helping people reconnect with culture, community, family, identity, purpose, recovery, and hope beyond survival.

Our Services & Action

What We Do

“

Island Warriors Street Outreach provides comprehensive, barrier-free support across Victoria through direct peer-led street outreach—meeting people where they are with food, clothing, water, hygiene supplies, and seasonal necessities. We offer deep Indigenous-led cultural support by connecting individuals with ceremonies, drumming, circles, Elders, and Knowledge Keepers for holistic healing. Our work encompasses specialized peer support from those who understand addiction, homelessness, and trauma firsthand, alongside dedicated recovery navigation to help individuals explore detox and treatment. We also provide systems advocacy, healthcare and housing referrals, community feeds, cultural workshops, and collaborative partnership building with nonprofits, governments, and community members to close gaps in services and foster lasting connection.

Sacred Philosophy

All My Relations

“

All My Relations is more than something we say—it reflects how we try to walk in this world.

It reminds us that we are connected to one another, our ancestors, future generations, our communities, and the land. That is why we don't see "the homeless" when we walk onto the street. We see our relatives. Someone's son. Someone's daughter. A mother, father, brother, sister, auntie, uncle, Elder, friend, or loved one.

Our doors and our hearts are open to people from all walks of life. Indigenous-led does not mean Indigenous-only. It means our work is guided by Indigenous values of relationship, responsibility, respect, reciprocity, and caring for one another.

"From the Streets to Serving the Streets — My Story of Addiction, Survival, Recovery, and Purpose"

My name is Shane Bullis, and some people know me as Bullet.

Before I tell you what Island Warriors Street Outreach Society is all about, I want to tell you a little bit about the man behind it and why this work means so much to me.

I'm not somebody who learned about addiction, homelessness, trauma, or incarceration from reading a textbook. I lived it.

For nearly 10 years, I was lost in addiction, homelessness, violence, and the streets. I lived a life that nearly killed me more times than I can count. I've been shot three times. I've jumped from a five-storey building. I've overdosed. I've sat behind jail walls. I've slept in places no human being should ever have to sleep. I've experienced the loneliness, shame, judgment, and hopelessness that so many of our unhoused relatives face every single day.

There was a time when I didn't believe I would ever make it out alive. I was using fentanyl heavily, destroying myself, and hurting the people who loved me. But Creator had other plans for me. Through recovery, ceremony, culture, prayer, and the love of people who refused to give up on me, I slowly began finding my way back. My Indigenous culture helped save my life.

On July 22, 2024, I began a new chapter in my life. Today, I have over two years of sobriety, and I wake up every morning grateful for another opportunity to do something meaningful with the life I was given. But getting sober wasn't the end of my journey; it was the beginning of my responsibility. If I ever made it out of that darkness, I made a promise to go back and help others find their way out too.

My name is Shane Bullis. I'm a father, a grandfather, a man in recovery, an Indigenous warrior, and the proud Founder and Executive Director of Island Warriors Street Outreach Society. We stand for our people. All My Relations. Miigwetch.

President & Co-Founder

Eddie Legault

A Story of Struggle, Survival, Recovery, and Giving Back. "I helped build this organization because I know what it's like to be homeless, to struggle with addiction, to feel forgotten, and to wonder whether anyone truly cares."

"A Story of Struggle, Survival, Recovery, and Giving Back"

My name is Eddie Legault. I'm 51 years old, and I was born in Sudbury, Ontario. My life hasn't been an easy road, growing up in foster care and group homes. When I was 13 or 14 years old, I moved to Victoria, British Columbia.

Like many people struggling with trauma and pain, I turned to substances at a young age, leading to homelessness, jail, and dark roads. In 2011, I attended Maple Ridge Treatment Centre, which became an important turning point. Over the years, I worked with Our Place Society in outreach and peer support.

My past doesn't define who I am today. It gives me the understanding, compassion, and determination to help others find their way. We do recover. Every story matters. All My Relations.
